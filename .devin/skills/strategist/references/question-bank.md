# Intake question bank

Pick the highest-impact questions for the case. Give a default for each. Skip any that can be inferred. Stay within the question limits of the chosen depth.

## Contents
- Universal questions
- New company
- New product or feature
- Geopolitical analysis or strategy
- Learning schedule
- Career planning
- Project or personal plan

## Universal questions

1. **Objective:** what exact outcome do you want, and by when? How will you know it succeeded (a number, a state, a date)?
2. **Motive:** what is the underlying reason you want this outcome? What changes for you if you get it, and if you do not?
3. **Constraints:** what are the hard limits on time, money, skills, energy, legal or ethical boundaries?
4. **Resources:** what do you already have (assets, skills, contacts, data, access)?
5. **Starting point:** where are you now relative to the goal? What have you already tried and what happened?
6. **Stakeholders and opposition:** who helps, who blocks, who is affected?
7. **Risk tolerance:** what is the worst outcome you can accept? Is a failure recoverable?
8. **Time horizon:** when must the first results appear, and when does the whole plan end?
9. **Non-negotiables:** what must be protected even if the plan suffers?
10. **Reversibility:** can you reverse the decision later, and at what cost?

## New company

- Problem and customer: who has the problem, how painful is it, how do they solve it today?
- Evidence: any customer conversations, pre-sales, data or prototypes?
- Founder fit: relevant skills, time available per week, financial runway in months.
- Funding: self-funded, raising, or revenue-first? Capital available and expected burn.
- Market: size estimate, competitors, why now.
- Unfair advantage: access, technology, cost, or distribution you have that others do not.
- Team and legal: co-founders, jurisdiction, regulatory exposure.
- Exit or goal: lifestyle business, venture scale, mission-driven?

## New product or feature

- Target user and the job they hire the product to do.
- Current product, metrics, and the problem this addresses (evidence).
- Resources: team size, build time, budget.
- Competitors and substitutes.
- Success metric and how it is measured.
- Launch constraints: dependencies, compliance, partners.
- Cost of being wrong and cost of delay.

## Geopolitical analysis or strategy

- Whose perspective or interests is the analysis for (a state, an organization, a researcher, an investor, a citizen)?
- Scope: region, actors, time horizon, specific question or decision.
- Tools in scope: diplomatic, economic, legal, informational, institutional. State any limits.
- Constraints: legal and ethical boundaries; capabilities and resources of the actor.
- What outcome counts as success and what trade-offs are acceptable.
- Key uncertainties the user already knows about.
- Required output: briefing, option paper, scenario analysis, decision recommendation.

## Learning schedule

- Target skill or exam, and the deadline if any.
- Current level and what you have already covered.
- Hours available per week (and realistic, not ideal) and fixed commitments.
- Learning preferences that have worked or failed before.
- Resources already owned or accessible; budget.
- How progress will be tested (exam, project, job requirement).
- Consistency history: how long past routines survived.

## Career planning

- Current role, skills, income needs, location flexibility.
- Target role or direction and the reason.
- Time and money available to retrain or switch.
- Market evidence: demand, entry requirements, salary range.
- Network and access to people in the target field.
- Risk tolerance and dependents or obligations.
- Fallback plan if the switch fails.

## Project or personal plan

- Deliverable and quality bar.
- Deadline and fixed milestones.
- Time available per week and when you work best.
- Dependencies and risks already known.
- Past similar projects: how long they really took.
